\abstract{
\textbf{Motivation:} Computational reconstruction of three-dimensional (3D) chromatin structures from chromosome conformation capture experiments
remains a computationally intensive task, particularly for large chromosomes and whole-genome analyses. Previous tools such as
3D-GNOME and its GPU-extended variant cudaMMC accelerated the heatmap-level Monte Carlo phase using CUDA kernels but left
the arc-distance and structural smoothing phases running on the CPU. This hybrid GPU--CPU design limits scalability
for datasets comprising many interaction blocks per chromosome.\\
\textbf{Results:} We present pMMC, a fully GPU-accelerated reimplementation of the multiscale Monte Carlo pipeline for 3D genome
structure modelling. pMMC offloads all three Monte Carlo phases --- heatmap positioning, arc-distance optimization,
and subanchor smoothing --- to dedicated CUDA kernels with warp-level simulated annealing. A persistent
\texttt{GpuResourceCache} eliminates repeated GPU memory allocation overhead across interaction blocks.
We benchmarked pMMC against cudaMMC and the original 3D-GNOME on three human chromosomes (chr1, chr14, chr21)
from merged ChIA-PET data, running 10 replicate reconstructions per tool. pMMC achieved mean wall-clock times of
4.92\,s (chr1), 4.27\,s (chr14), and 3.96\,s (chr21), compared with 7.49\,s, 5.27\,s, and 4.24\,s for cudaMMC, and
67.0\,s, 14.7\,s, and 4.56\,s for 3D-GNOME, respectively. The speedup of pMMC over cudaMMC scales with chromosome
size: 1.52$\times$ for chr1 but only 1.07$\times$ for chr21, which indicates that the GPU-offloaded phases
contribute more substantially for larger inputs. Output structure quality, measured by pairwise Pearson correlation
of distance matrices, was $r = 1.000$ between pMMC and cudaMMC across all chromosomes, confirming that pMMC
preserves numerical equivalence of the reconstruction.\\
\textbf{Availability and implementation:} Source code, benchmark data, and documentation are available at
\url{https://github.com/SFGLab/pMMC}. pMMC is implemented in C++17/CUDA and builds on Linux with CMake.\\
\textbf{Contact:} \href{email:d.plewczynski@cent.uw.edu.pl}{d.plewczynski@cent.uw.edu.pl}}

\keywords{3D genome, chromatin structure, Monte Carlo, CUDA, GPU, ChIA-PET}

\section{Introduction}

Spatial organisation of chromatin inside the cell nucleus plays a central role in the regulation
of gene expression, DNA replication and repair \citep{zheng2018threed}. Chromosome conformation capture
techniques, including Hi-C and ChIA-PET, provide pairwise interaction frequencies that can be converted
into spatial restraints for 3D structure modelling \citep{rousseau2011threed}. Polymer-physics-based
approaches describe chromatin as a bead-spring chain and employ stochastic optimisation to satisfy
distance restraints derived from these interaction data \citep{rosa2016polymer}.

Several tools have been developed for this purpose. 3D-GNOME \citep{szalaj2016threed, szalaj2023threed3}
implements a multiscale Monte Carlo algorithm that reconstructs chromatin structures at four hierarchical
levels --- whole genome, chromosome, segment (topologically associating domain), and interaction
block --- using simulated annealing at each resolution. Loop extrusion and compartmental segregation
provide complementary biological frameworks for interpreting the resulting structures
\citep{bohnuud2018formation, nuebler2018chromatin}. cudaMMC \citep{saqib2023cudammc} accelerated the
coarsest Monte Carlo phase (heatmap-level positioning) on the GPU, while arc-distance and structural
smoothing remained on the CPU.

Several recent methods also model chromatin at multiple scales. MultiMM \citep{neguembor2024multimm}
builds coarse-grained to nucleosome-resolution models; LoopSage \citep{sakaue2024loopsage} uses
energy-based Monte Carlo for loop extrusion; the integrated 3D-GNOME engine
\citep{plewczynski2026integrated} extends the original web service with GPU support. Earlier work
demonstrated that GPU-based Monte Carlo sampling can provide substantial acceleration for polymer
simulations \citep{lee2010utility, lutz2012massively}, and population-based genome structure analysis
has been used to study the forces driving spatial organisation
\citep{collepardo2020population}.

Here we present pMMC, a complete GPU-accelerated reimplementation. pMMC offloads all three Monte Carlo phases
to CUDA kernels with warp-level parallelism, introduces a persistent GPU resource cache that avoids repeated
memory allocation, and adds several features including MDS-based initialisation, multiple output formats
(mmCIF, PDB), and a built-in benchmarking framework with structural quality metrics.

\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{3D-GNOME --- CPU-oriented approach}

The original 3D-GNOME pipeline \citep{szalaj2016threed} models chromatin as a four-level hierarchical tree of
beads: whole chromosome, segments (${\sim}\,2$\,Mb), interaction blocks bounded by ChIA-PET anchors, and
subanchor beads interpolated between anchors. At each level the algorithm converts interaction frequencies to
expected spatial distances using a power-law mapping ($d = a \cdot f^{-c}$), constructs a distance matrix,
and applies simulated annealing Monte Carlo optimisation to minimise the discrepancy between actual and
expected inter-bead distances. The energy function includes distance-based, loop-energy, structural
smoothness, and orientation scoring components. All computations in the original 3D-GNOME run on the CPU, and
the three Monte Carlo phases are executed sequentially for every interaction block in every segment.

\subsection{cudaMMC approach --- GPU-extended}

cudaMMC \citep{saqib2023cudammc} introduced a GPU kernel (\texttt{ParallelMonteCarloHeatmap}) for the first
Monte Carlo phase, which positions chromosome territories and segments using singleton heatmap data. This
kernel launches multiple CUDA threads that each perform independent simulated annealing trajectories, with FP16
bead positions and FP32 energy evaluation, and selects the best-scoring trajectory at the end. A second
kernel (\texttt{ParallelMarkArcs}) parallelises the mapping of genomic-coordinate arcs to anchor indices.
However, the arc-distance and smoothing Monte Carlo phases remain on the CPU in cudaMMC. For larger
chromosomes with many interaction blocks (e.g.\ chr1 with 125 segments), these CPU-bound phases dominate
the total runtime.

\subsection{pMMC approach --- GPU-oriented, optimised, and additional features}

pMMC replaces the CPU arc-distance and smoothing phases with two new CUDA kernels and introduces several
architectural improvements.

\paragraph{ParallelMonteCarloArcs kernel.}
This kernel implements warp-level simulated annealing for anchor positioning. Each warp (32 threads) runs an
independent SA trajectory: thread~0 selects beads and proposes random displacements, while all 32 threads
cooperate on energy evaluation using warp-shuffle reduction. The energy function penalises the squared
relative deviation of inter-bead distances from expected values derived from PET cluster counts, with
separate spring constants for stretched and compressed distances. The grid is sized to
$N_\text{SM}$ blocks of 128 threads (4 warps per block), where $N_\text{SM}$ is the number of streaming
multiprocessors. Shared memory stores per-warp position arrays. After convergence, the positions from the
best-scoring warp are transferred back to the host.

\paragraph{ParallelMonteCarloSmooth kernel.}
This kernel smooths the subanchor bead chain using local energy evaluation. Each block contains a single
warp (32 threads) and processes one smoothing trajectory. The energy function sums bond-length penalties
(spring model with stretch and squeeze constants) and angular bending penalties (proportional to
$1 - \cos\theta$ at each bead triple). Because only bonds and angles involving the moved bead need
recomputation, each step costs $O(1)$ instead of $O(n)$. The grid uses $2 \times N_\text{SM}$ blocks.
Milestone-based convergence detection terminates the SA when score improvement stalls for a configurable
number of consecutive checkpoints.

\paragraph{GpuResourceCache.}
A critical optimisation in pMMC is the \texttt{GpuResourceCache} class, which allocates curandState arrays,
isDone flags, and working buffers once at programme startup and reuses them across all interaction blocks
within and between chromosomes. In cudaMMC, every invocation of the heatmap kernel incurred a
\texttt{cudaMalloc}/\texttt{cudaFree} cycle for random-number-generator states and output arrays. For chr1,
which contains 125 interaction blocks and therefore triggers ${\sim}\,284$ kernel calls, this repeated
allocation overhead was substantial. The cache eliminates it entirely, yielding especially large gains for
chromosomes with many segments.

\paragraph{Additional features.}
pMMC supports MDS-based initialisation of bead positions via classical Torgerson multidimensional scaling, as
an alternative to random placement. It exports reconstructed structures in mmCIF and PDB formats in addition
to the native HCM format. A built-in benchmarking framework (\texttt{BenchmarkRunner}) with synthetic data
generation and structural quality metrics (RMSD, Pearson~$r$, stratum-adjusted correlation) enables
systematic evaluation. Deterministic reproducibility is ensured through controlled seeding of both CPU and
GPU random-number generators.

\subsection{Chromatin interaction data}

We used merged ChIA-PET data from blood lymphoblast cells for three human chromosomes of varying size:
chr1 (125 segments), chr14 (54 segments), and chr21 (25 segments). Anchors, PET clusters, singletons,
and segment boundaries were provided in standard BED/BEDPE format. All tools used the same input data and,
where applicable, equivalent configuration parameters for simulated annealing schedules.

\section{Comparison of performance between pMMC vs cudaMMC and 3D-GNOME}

We benchmarked three tools --- 3D-GNOME (denoted 3dome\_mmc), cudaMMC, and pMMC --- on the same hardware
(NVIDIA RTX~4070, 12\,GB VRAM, 46 SMs; AMD Ryzen 7 CPU, 32\,GB RAM) using 10 replicate runs per
tool per chromosome with GPU utilisation and memory monitored continuously.

\paragraph{Wall-clock time.}
Table~\ref{tab:walltime} reports mean wall-clock times. 3D-GNOME was the slowest in all cases, reaching
67.0\,s for chr1 where CPU-only Monte Carlo over 125 segments dominates. cudaMMC reduced this to 7.49\,s
via the GPU heatmap kernel, but the CPU-bound arc and smooth phases still contributed the majority of time
for larger chromosomes. pMMC further reduced the chr1 runtime to 4.92\,s --- a 1.52$\times$ speedup over
cudaMMC and a 13.6$\times$ speedup over 3D-GNOME. For the smallest chromosome (chr21), the improvement
over cudaMMC was marginal (1.07$\times$), because the arc and smooth phases constitute a smaller fraction
of the total time when there are only 25 segments.

\paragraph{Scalability.}
Figure~\ref{fig:scaling} shows the scaling regression and whole-genome scalability projection. The
ratio of chr1 to chr21 wall time reveals the scaling behaviour: 3D-GNOME exhibits a ratio of 14.7$\times$,
cudaMMC 1.77$\times$, and pMMC 1.24$\times$. The near-flat profile of pMMC indicates that its
runtime is dominated by a fixed overhead (GPU initialisation, I/O) rather than by computation that scales
with the number of interaction blocks. This characteristic makes pMMC especially suitable for whole-genome
reconstruction, where many large chromosomes must be processed.

\paragraph{Output quality.}
The pairwise Pearson correlation of distance matrices between pMMC and cudaMMC outputs was
$r = 1.000$ for all three chromosomes, indicating numerically identical structures when the same random
seed is used. Intra-tool consistency was also $r = 1.000$ for pMMC across different runs with the
same seed, confirming full deterministic reproducibility. Figure~\ref{fig:quality} shows the output
quality heatmap.
Beyond single-model equivalence, we assessed structural consistency across 50-model ensembles
for chr1. The median pairwise Spearman correlation of distance matrices was $\rho = 0.86$ for pMMC
ensembles versus $\rho = 0.59$ for cudaMMC, indicating that pMMC produces more structurally coherent
model populations. Ensemble generation was also faster: pMMC completed 50 models in 243\,s compared
with 375\,s for cudaMMC and 3307\,s for 3D-GNOME, corresponding to a $1.54\times$ speedup over
cudaMMC and $13.6\times$ over 3D-GNOME at ensemble scale.

Table~\ref{tab:resources} summarises GPU and memory statistics. All three tools use comparable peak VRAM
(${\sim}\,760$--$1050$\,MB). Peak RSS is ${\sim}\,12$\,MB for 3D-GNOME (which barely uses the GPU) vs.\

kernel runs for only tens to hundreds of milliseconds before returning control to the CPU for the next

The coefficient of variation (CV) of wall-clock time was below 8\% for all tools across all chromosomes

slightly higher variance (6.2\% for chr1) due to GPU scheduling non-determinism.

\section{Conclusions}

We have presented pMMC, a fully GPU-accelerated tool for multiscale Monte Carlo reconstruction of 3D
chromatin structures. By offloading all three SA phases to CUDA kernels and employing a persistent
GPU resource cache, pMMC achieves up to 1.52$\times$ speedup over cudaMMC and 13.6$\times$ over the
original CPU-based 3D-GNOME, with the advantage growing for larger chromosomes. Output quality is
numerically identical to cudaMMC ($r = 1.000$), and 50-model ensembles exhibit higher structural
consistency (median Spearman $\rho = 0.86$ vs.\ $0.59$). pMMC further offers MDS
initialisation, mmCIF/PDB export, and built-in benchmarking. These improvements make pMMC a practical
tool for whole-genome-scale 3D structure modelling from ChIA-PET data.
